Construction at this site has continued for nearly five years, causing severe air and noise pollution in our home. My daughter is now 7 years old and has been exposed to continuous dust and noise from the age of 2, during the most critical years of lung and brain development.

We understand that construction is a normal part of a growing city, and for a long time we tolerated the inconvenience without complaint. We are writing now because it has become a health concern for our family. The constant dust is affecting our respiratory health, especially our child's, and the continuous noise disrupts our daily routine, sleep and work, causing significant stress at home. This complaint is first and foremost about protecting our family's health, beyond any regulatory technicality.

Observed issues (video evidence dated 02-Nov-2026 attached):
1. A large excavated area with exposed soil left uncovered.
2. No water sprinkling or other dust suppression observed.
3. Barricading present on only part of the site perimeter.
4. Heavy rock-breaking and excavation work, causing severe noise and ground vibration in adjacent homes.
5. Continuous high-noise work [add if true: including late evenings / Sundays].

Construction dust contains PM10, PM2.5 and crystalline silica, which is a known carcinogen. Children are especially vulnerable to respiratory harm from prolonged exposure, and constant noise affects their sleep and learning.

I respectfully request:
1. A joint site inspection by TGPCB and GHMC officials, including measurement of ambient noise and particulate (PM10, PM2.5) levels at the site boundary and at adjacent homes.
2. Verification that the site has installed the approved PM10/PM2.5 sensors connected to TGPCB, as mandated for construction projects above 500 square metres, and a review of the recorded data.
3. Enforcement of dust control under the Construction & Demolition Waste Management Rules, 2016: covering of exposed soil and materials, regular water sprinkling and full barricading.
4. Restriction of noisy work to permitted daytime hours, and compliance with residential-area noise limits, under the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000.
5. Verification of the building permit and its validity period, given the length of construction.
6. A committed completion timeline from the builder.

I request a written acknowledgement and a complaint reference number.
